Dear Editors-in-Chief, Empirical Software Engineering

I submit the manuscript "Explanation Context and Bug-Repair Calibration in Open-Weight Code LLMs on
Novice Python Tasks" for consideration as a research article reporting a negative result.

The study asks whether an explanation placed in the prompt - accurate or misleading - changes how
well open-weight language models repair novice-level Python bugs and how confident they report
being. It contributes (1) a benchmark of 40 seeded bugs whose tests were validated with seeded
faults, alternative solutions and generated mutants; (2) a controlled four-condition comparison over
1,440 runs of three local models; and (3) a calibration analysis. The main findings are a null
result for the hypothesised harm of misleading explanations, severe over-confidence of a 1.5B model
that returned buggy code unchanged in almost half of its runs, and the observation that only 12 of
40 model-written explanations of buggy code were fully accurate.

The work fits Empirical Software Engineering because it is an empirical evaluation of LLM-based
program repair with a validated test oracle, a pre-specified analysis and full open data, and
because the journal solicits negative results. It complements recent articles in the journal on
LLM-based repair (Alhanahnah et al., 2025) and on the effect of prompt design in LLM-based test
generation (Ouédraogo et al., 2026), and on LLM-based detection and repair of vulnerable code (Belozerov et al., 2026).

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has no human participants.
Code, data and logs are public at https://github.com/sultansmailov3/explainbench (release `final`).

the content. I have no conflict of interest to declare. [Funding statement.]
